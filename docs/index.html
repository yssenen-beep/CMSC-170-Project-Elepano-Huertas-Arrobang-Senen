Problem & Motivation

Constructing a conflict-free weekly class schedule requires satisfying degree curriculum requirements, staying within the 21-unit academic cap (and 5-unit HK/NSTP cap), avoiding overlapping sections, and respecting student time preferences such as blocked vacant hours and desired inter-class gaps.

This study benchmarks three classical AI paradigms—Backtracking, A* Search, and a Genetic Algorithm—across runtime, schedule validity, and preference satisfaction.

AMIS Data Pipeline

  • 1. Formatting: Parsed raw single-line JSON dumps (first_semester.json, second_semester.json, plan_of_study.json) into structured CSV tables.
  • 2. Cleaning: Stripped non-CSP metadata (enrollment counts, instructors) to retain course codes, sections, units, and meeting slots.
  • 3. Filtering: Scoped curricula and schedules to UPLB College of Engineering and Agro-Industrial Technology (CEAT) programs.
  • 4. Simplifying: Deduplicated identical course sections sharing the same time slot under the relaxed unit-scarcity assumption.

Algorithm Formulations & Hyperparameters

MethodSearch & Optimization MechanismPreference HandlingHyperparameters / Budget
Backtracking Recursive depth-first CSP search ordering required courses first, then optional specialization/elective/GE pools by fewest available sections (MRV heuristic), with unit-reachability pruning. Scores candidates on a 0–100 preference scale combining vacant-time avoidance and target-gap tolerance; stops early when max reachable units and 100% preference score are met.
A* Search Best-first search minimizing f(n) = g(n) + h(n), where g(n) penalizes gap cost (0.01/min) and blocked slots (+1 per slot) and h(n) is the admissible remaining academic unit shortfall to 21 units. Supports both minimum-gap mode (preferred_gap = 0) and target-gap deviation minimization alongside soft vacant-block penalties.
Genetic Algorithm Evolves chromosomes mapping courses to sections using elitism (top 1 carried over), 3-way tournament selection, uniform random crossover, and random section/drop mutation. Optimizes the shared fitness function directly (penalizing conflicts, excess/missing units, total gap minutes, and blocked slots).

Scope & Limitations

  • Single-College Scope: All datasets and evaluations are restricted to undergraduate degree programs under the College of Engineering and Agro-Industrial Technology (CEAT) at UPLB.
  • Exclusion of Faculty Preferences: Schedules are optimized solely on curriculum requirements, unit caps, and time preferences without accounting for student preferences for specific Faculty-in-Charge (FIC).
  • Assumption of Unlimited Class Capacity: The simulation omits real-time slot scarcity, section caps, and enlistment competition, assuming a student can enroll in any selected section.
  • No Fixed-Subject Anchoring: Schedules are generated from scratch for the selected term without allowing users to lock in pre-selected sections or mandatory break blocks prior to search.
